Architecture & Treasures

Palaces of Opulence & History

Behind Mehrangarh's austere sandstone fortifications lies a labyrinth of exquisitely carved courtyards, gilded halls, and one of the finest royal museums across Asia.

24K Gold Filigree
Maharaja Abhay Singh (1724–49)

Phool Mahal (Palace of Flowers)

The most flamboyant and lavish hall in the fortress. Created as an exclusive pleasure chamber for classical music and dance recitals, featuring gold leaf brought directly from Gujarat after victory in Ahmedabad.

Belgian Convex Mirrors
Royal Bedchamber of Ajit Singh

Sheesh Mahal (Hall of Mirrors)

Inlaid with shimmering mirror mosaics, intricate floral stucco, and colorful glass panels depicting scenes from the Ramayana and Lord Krishna dancing with Gopis, illuminated magically by a single candle flame.

Chunnam Pearl Plaster
Hall of Public Audience

Moti Mahal (Pearl Palace)

Built by Raja Sawai Sur Singh. Its polished seashell plaster sparkles like fine pearl. Above the hall sit five concealed jharokha balconies allowing the queens (purdah) to listen unseen to court deliberations.

Silver Elephant Seats
Royal Armoury & Regalia

Daulat Khana & Royal Howdahs

Houses the world's most magnificent collection of ornate elephant carriages, including the solid silver howdah presented by Mughal Emperor Shah Jahan to Maharaja Jaswant Singh I in the 17th century.

31 Sati Handprints
15th Century Gateway

Loha Pol (The Iron Gate)

The final defensive gate leading into the inner royal sanctuary. Its stone jambs bear the solemn, vermillion-stained handprints of royal Rathore queens who committed Sati before funeral pyres in 1843.

Cannon Battery
410 Ft Sheer Drop

The Grand Ramparts & Artillery

Lined with historic brass and iron artillery pieces such as Kilkila, Shambhu Ban, and Gajnak, offering unrivaled 360-degree views over Jodhpur's azure houses, Jaswant Thada, and the Thar desert horizon.

560+ Years of Valor

Chronicles of Mehrangarh

From its foundation on the Mountain of Birds to its renaissance under the Mehrangarh Museum Trust.

1459 AD

The Foundation by Rao Jodha

Rao Jodha, 15th chief of the Rathore clan, shifts his seat from the vulnerable Mandore to the formidable, isolated rocky plateau known as Bhakurcheeria (the Mountain of Birds), laying the foundation of Jodhpur.

1544 AD

The Siege of Sher Shah Suri

Sher Shah Suri launches a massive campaign against Marwar. Following the hard-fought battle of Sammel, Suri remarked: "For a handful of bajra (millet), I nearly lost the empire of Hindustan." The fort withstands the onslaught.

1707 AD

Maharaja Ajit Singh Reclaims the Citadel

After decades of guerrilla resistance led by the legendary patriot Veer Durgadas Rathore, Maharaja Ajit Singh ousts Mughal garrisons, embarking on an era of magnificent palace construction and marble restorations.

1806 AD

Construction of Jai Pol (Victory Gate)

Maharaja Man Singh builds the monumental Jai Pol to celebrate crushing triumphs over the combined armies of Jaipur and Bikaner. Cannon ball scars from this famous siege remain visibly embedded in the outer ramparts.

1972 AD

Establishment of Museum Trust

His Highness Maharaja Gaj Singh II dedicates the fort's historic collections to the public by founding the Mehrangarh Museum Trust, creating one of the world's most meticulously preserved living heritage sites.

Present Day

Global Cultural Capital

Mehrangarh serves as the grand stage for the internationally acclaimed Rajasthan International Folk Festival (RIFF) and the World Sacred Spirit Festival, uniting master artists from the Thar Desert and across the globe.

Desert Lore

Myths, Curses & Sacrifice

Beneath the towering sandstone walls of Mehrangarh echo tales of ancient hermits, royal curses, and eternal devotion.

The Hermit's Curse: Cheeria Nathji

The hill of Bhakurcheeria was the secluded hermitage of a yogi named Cheeria Nathji. When forced to vacate by Rao Jodha, the enraged hermit cursed the citadel: "May your kingdom suffer eternal droughts of water!" To this day, Jodhpur experiences water scarcity every few years.

The Supreme Sacrifice of Raja Ram Meghwal

To appease astrological omens and counteract the hermit's curse, a brave subject named Raja Ram Meghwal voluntarily agreed to be buried alive inside the fort's foundation trench in 1459. His descendants were granted royal patronage and estates in perpetuity.

Chamunda Devi: The Guardian of 1965

During the 1965 war between India and Pakistan, Pakistani warplanes targeted Jodhpur. Citizens recount that a swarm of eagles (the divine avatar of goddess Chamunda) formed an aerial canopy over the citadel and city, deflecting airborne bombings.

Practical Information

Plan Your Visit to the Sun City

Everything you need to know for an unforgettable journey to Rajasthan's crown jewel.

Operating Hours

Open daily from 09:00 AM to 05:00 PM throughout the year, including national holidays. Sunset ramparts viewing closes at 05:30 PM.

Best Season

October to March offers pleasant desert breezes and cool mornings (15°C–25°C). October also hosts the vibrant RIFF folk festival.

How to Reach

Located in central Jodhpur. Auto-rickshaws and cabs ascend the fort hill road easily. A stone pedestrian pathway also winds up from the old blue town (Navchokiya).

Guided Tours

High-fidelity audio guides are available in 7 global languages. Highly knowledgeable royal trust guides can also be engaged at the ticket counter.

Accessibility & Lift

Modern passenger elevators transport elderly and mobility-impaired visitors directly from the base gate to the top palatial courtyards.

Desert Rock Park

Adjacent to the fort lies the 70-hectare Rao Jodha Desert Rock Park, showcasing ancient volcanic rhyolite rock formations and Thar flora.
